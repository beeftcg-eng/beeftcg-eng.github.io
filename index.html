Pairings

A tracker for local TCG tournaments. It keeps your events, your results round by round, and your decks in one place, and it tells your friends where you're playing next.

  • Stats that answer real questions: win rate by deck and by month, the matchups you beat and the ones you lose to, and streaks.
  • Social: friends, playgroups, store pages with QR check-in, shareable event links, and push notifications when friends are going.
  • Works offline: edits queue up and merge per item when you're back online, so another device's changes are never overwritten.
  • Fully bilingual (English and Spanish), with automated checks for missing translations and for color contrast.

TurnZero

A shared round timer for TCG events. The judge controls the clock, and every player sees it live on their phone or on the store's TV.

  • No accounts, nothing to install: the judge gets a private control link, and players open a view link, scan a QR code, or type a 4-character code.
  • Optional push alerts at 15 minutes in, 10 and 5 minutes left, extra time, and time's up.
  • Tested end to end, including in a real browser and against a real database.

Beef's Brewhouse

A deckbuilder for five games: Riftbound, One Piece, Pokémon, Magic: The Gathering and Yu-Gi-Oh!. It has card images, legality checking against each game's real format rules, and your collection built in.

  • Each game's real rules: Riftbound's Legend and runes, One Piece's Leader, Commander's color identity, Yu-Gi-Oh!'s Extra Deck and ban lists.
  • Collection, wishlist and trading: it shows what each deck is still missing and matches your wants with other players' trade binders.
  • One app on desktop and phone, with local-first sync between them, automatic backups, and updates that install themselves.
  • Shows each deck's tournament record from Pairings.

Pawmodoro

A pen-and-paper style desktop app that puts notes, a recurring checklist, a pomodoro timer and ambient sound in one window, with XP and quests to make the routine stick.

  • One codebase, native on Linux and Windows, with a proper installer (built for winget) and an in-app updater that checks each download's checksum.
  • Ambient sounds synthesized from code: rain, ocean, café, fireplace. No recordings, so no licensing problems.
  • Optional cloud sync and a phone app, plus a shared household list for two people's groceries and chores, with reminders.
  • XP rules the server can't disagree with: the same levels and quests are computed on the desktop and in the database.

How they fit together

Separate apps with separate backends, linked on purpose, so each piece of data has one home.

Brewhouse⇄Pairings

Decks flow into Pairings with their legend and card count, and each deck's tournament record flows back into Brewhouse. Each app reads from the other; neither one writes to it.

Brewhouse⇄Pawmodoro

Brewhouse signs in with your Pawmodoro account. Your wishlist can go straight onto the Pawmodoro checklist, and your decks sync between desktop and phone.

Pairings→TurnZero

Every event card in Pairings, and every store owner's dashboard, links straight to TurnZero, so starting a round clock for the room is one tap away.

How I build

The same habits show up in every project.

Local-first, never lose an edit

Changes save on the device first and upload in the background. A version check or an outbox makes sure an old copy never overwrites a newer one.

Security in the database

Row-level security on every table, and privileged columns protected by triggers. Only public keys ship in the apps, and the policies are checked by automated tests.

Tests, plus honest notes

Each app has a test suite, and schema changes are checked against a real Postgres. Every release says plainly what couldn't be tested live.

Shipping is part of the product

Real installers, apps that update themselves, and phone apps that install from a link. Nobody needs a terminal to get or update anything.
